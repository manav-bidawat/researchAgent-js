**Answer**

The papers **do not agree** on this point. There is a clear split:

**Most papers state that removing parameters typically requires retraining:**
- 2502_01705v4 states that structured pruning "removes entire components to enhance efficiency but often causes performance drops, requiring retraining" [2502_01705v4__c0013].
- 2412_11629v1 (QPruner) says "most real-time systems require dynamic adaptation, which means that the pruned model must be fine-tuned to improve performance" [2412_11629v1__c0008], and that "fine-tuning is often used to recover the accuracy of pruned models" [2412_11629v1__c0003].
- Even 2505_18232v4 acknowledges that prior methods "directly remove unimportant parameters based on certain metrics, which often causes knowledge loss and necessitates extensive retraining" [2505_18232v4__c0001].

**One paper claims its method avoids retraining entirely:**
- 2505_18232v4 (TRSP) argues that its two-stage regularization approach transfers knowledge from layers being removed to the remaining layers *before* pruning, so "the model can maintain good language modeling capability" without post-pruning fine-tuning [2505_18232v4__c0004]. It explicitly states "TRSP is retraining free after pruning" [2505_18232v4__c0004] and notes that while baselines were retrained with LoRA, "we did not retrain TRSP" [2505_18232v4__c0018].

**The disagreement stems from methodology:** Traditional approaches remove parameters directly and then fine-tune to recover performance. TRSP proposes applying regularization first to shift knowledge to surviving layers, claiming this makes post-pruning retraining unnecessary. The other papers do not address this specific technique and instead describe the conventional workflow where retraining follows parameter removal.

> **Across the compression papers, what different strategies are used to avoid an expensive retraining stage after parameters are removed?**

**Answer**

Across the compression papers, several distinct strategies are used to avoid expensive retraining after parameters are removed:

**1. Regularization-Based Knowledge Transfer (TRSP)**
TRSP applies two-stage regularization before pruning to preserve model performance without retraining [2505_18232v4__c0001]. In the first stage, learnable weights multiply transformer layer outputs while their ℓ1-norm is incorporated as a regularization term in the loss function [2505_18232v4__c0004]. In the second stage, additional regularization is applied to the difference between layer inputs and outputs for layers with smaller weights, which forces important knowledge to be transferred to the remaining layers [2505_18232v4__c0004]. This systematic approach ensures that "the model can maintain good language modeling capability" and is explicitly described as "retraining free after pruning" [2505_18232v4__c0004][2505_18232v4__c0035].

**2. Residual Approximation in Post-Training Frameworks**
Several post-training quantization (PTQ) methods use residual approximation to compensate for parameter removal without backpropagation. BiLLM introduces a residual approximation strategy to enhance 1-bit LLM performance [2502_01705v4__c0004], while ARB-LLM adopts an alternating refinement approach to better align binarized weights with their full-precision counterparts [2502_01705v4__c0004]. These PTQ techniques eliminate the need for gradient backpropagation, making them faster and less resource-demanding than Quantization-Aware Training (QAT), though generally resulting in lower accuracy [2412_11629v1__c0026][2502_01705v4__c0009].

**3. Progressive Pruning with Joint Optimization (PBS2P)**
PBS2P uses Stepwise Semi-structured Pruning with Binarization Optimization (SPBO), which progressively prunes a subset of elements at each step while jointly optimizing binarized parameters [2502_01705v4__c0002][2502_01705v4__c0015]. Simultaneous optimization of both the pruning mask and binarized parameters is NP-hard, so SPBO adopts a greedy-style approximation: at each step, a small portion of elements is pruned, then the binarized parameters are updated to fit the current pruning ratio [2502_01705v4__c0015][2502_01705v4__c0017]. This step-by-step strategy distributes optimization across multiple pruning stages, reducing cumulative error from both pruning and binarization [2502_01705v4__c0007][2502_01705v4__c0017].

**4. Hessian-Based Second-Order Error Compensation**
PBS2P employs a theoretically-grounded element selection criterion based on second-order Taylor approximation. The pruning score s_q = w²_q / [H⁻¹]qq minimizes error increase under second-order approximation, where [H⁻¹]qq is the q-th diagonal entry of the inverse Hessian matrix [2502_01705v4__c0022]. This criterion is "theoretically guaranteed to minimize the error increase introduced by pruning under a second-order approximation" [2502_01705v4__c0022]. The method also uses a Coarse-to-Fine Search (CFS): layer importance determines pruning ratios per layer, then a Hessian-based metric identifies the most redundant elements within each layer [2502_01705v4__c0002][2502_01705v4__c0007].

**5. Adaptive Feature Retention Score Aggregation (AFR)**
AFR combines ReFer (feature-space preservation) and SNIP (task-specific gradient-based) scores through standardization and summation to identify important parameters without training [2607_08027v1__c0004]. ReFer measures how each weight affects feature space geometry captured during pre-training, while SNIP uses the product of loss gradients and weights to measure contribution to task-specific loss [2607_08027v1__c0004][2607_08027v1__c0005]. When adapted to structured pruning, three challenges arise: distribution mismatch between heterogeneous scores, loss of sign information, and outlier influence. The solution combines power transformation for nonlinear distribution alignment, sign-preserving aggregation to evaluate optimization direction consistency, and percentile-based outlier removal (2nd–98th percentiles) for robust aggregation [2607_08027v1__c0003][2607_08027v1__c0019]. This enables structured pruning comparable to unstructured AFR while achieving practical inference speedup (1.56–1.57× at 50% pruning) [2607_08027v1__c0001][2607_08027v1__c0028].

**6. Sample-Aware Calibration Data Selection (AdaPruner)**
AdaPruner uses Bayesian optimization to adaptively search for optimal calibration data and importance estimation metrics simultaneously, avoiding poor pruning decisions caused by suboptimal calibration samples [2503_06184v1__c0002][2503_06184v1__c0020]. Importance is estimated via Taylor expansion measuring the impact of parameter removal on token prediction loss [2503_06184v1__c0005]. The pruned model retains 93.3% of average zero-shot performance without fine-tuning, outperforming LLM-Pruner's 89.8% [2503_06184v1__c0019]. Even with randomized calibration data, AdaPruner still outperforms baselines, demonstrating robustness [2503_06184v1__c0020].

**7. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) for Recovery**
When some recovery is needed, PEFT methods like LoRA insert trainable rank decomposition matrices parallel to pre-trained weights, keeping original parameters frozen [2412_11629v1__c0027]. LoftQ extends this by iteratively updating low-rank matrices such that Q + AB approximates the full-precision matrix W, providing better initialization for LoRA fine-tuning of quantized models [2412_11629v1__c0027]. These methods dramatically reduce memory requirements compared to full-parameter fine-tuning, though full fine-tuning would theoretically perform better [2412_11629v1__c0023][2412_11629v1__c0027].
